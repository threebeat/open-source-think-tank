# Community standards and moderation contract

**Status:** Canonical v2 product requirement. Counsel must review final enforceable terms before production launch.

## Purpose

Commonhall protects wide political disagreement while enforcing the behavioral and procedural floor needed for participation. Moderators judge conduct, safety, submission completeness, and published qualification criteria. They do not judge whether an opinion is popular, conventional, left, right, centrist, religious, secular, radical, or personally agreeable.

## Joining commitments

Every community member must assent to a versioned, plain-language standard that prohibits:

- credible threats, incitement, targeted harassment, stalking, or intimidation;
- doxxing or exposure of private, sensitive, or security-relevant information;
- hate or dehumanization directed at a person or group based on protected or analogous characteristics, including racism, sexism, and other discriminatory abuse;
- sexual harassment or non-consensual sexual content;
- impersonation, fraud, coordinated inauthentic participation, vote manipulation, or evasion of an active restriction;
- spam, malware, credential collection, or destructive interference with the service;
- unlawful content that the service is legally required to remove; and
- repeated disruption that prevents others from participating after clear notice and proportionate intervention.

Disagreement, criticism of institutions or ideologies, discussion of controversial policy, evidence of unequal outcomes, quotation for analysis, and good-faith challenges to community norms are not violations merely because they cause discomfort. Context, target, pattern, severity, and credible harm matter.

## Commons information architecture

Formal categories appear first:

1. Moderator communications
2. Council communications
3. Qualified topic discussions
4. Qualified approach discussions
5. Community actions

Then show this persistent boundary before informal categories:

> Informal conversations may not have been reviewed by a moderator and have not qualified for community deliberation. Their presence does not mean the organization endorses their claims, evidence, or conduct. Report rule-breaking content; challenge ideas without attacking people.

Informal categories:

1. Topic proposals
2. Approach proposals
3. General discussion
4. Disqualified Topics (honorable loss of qualification only)

“Formal” means a moderator confirmed the applicable process and safety criteria. It never means the moderator, Chamber, Council, nonprofit, or service agrees with the content.

## Two independent moderator decisions

Qualification and safety are recorded separately.

| Axis | Question | Allowed result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Safety | Does the content violate a versioned community/service rule? | clear, restrict, remove, escalate |
| Qualification | Does the submission satisfy the published topic/approach criteria? | qualify, return for revision, wait for evidence/duplicates/capacity |

A topic cannot be rejected for its viewpoint. A moderator must select objective criteria, point to the relevant content or missing field, and provide a short explanation. There is no “I disagree,” “too controversial,” “bad politics,” or ideology field.

## Required controls

- Reason codes are versioned and narrow; free text supplements rather than replaces them.
- Moderators recuse from cases involving a personal conflict, targeted abuse against themselves, or substantive advocacy they led.
- The system prevents a moderator from deciding their own proposal or appeal.
- Serious or ambiguous cases require dual control; emergencies may hide content temporarily but receive prompt independent review.
- The author can see the rule version, decision, evidence considered, public/private effect, duration, appeal path, and reviewer role.
- Appeals go to a different qualified reviewer or panel and produce a linked outcome.
- Restorations and reversals are as auditable as removals.
- Public transparency uses safe aggregates and reason categories; it never republishes harmful content or private moderator notes.
- Calibration review samples decisions across viewpoints and protected groups without inferring ideology from consultation data.
- Submission rate limits and queue capacity apply consistently. Elevated status never buys preferential qualification.

## Disqualification

**Honorable loss of qualification** is not punishment. It means a qualified topic did not meet a published time, engagement, duplication, scope, or remediable quality rule. The immutable topic moves to Informal Commons → Disqualified Topics with its safe history and published aggregate consultation insights.

**Dishonorable loss of qualification** is reserved for a serious ethics or service-rule breach. The topic is dead, is not listed in Disqualified Topics, and unsafe content is not republished. A protected audit entry, notice, and appeal remain. Public transparency may show a safe aggregate reason category only when doing so does not amplify harm or identify a target.

In either case, a future proposal is a new topic. It may link the old record when public and safe, but it receives a new review, rule trace, topic ID, and Pol.is entity.

## Qualification criteria floor

Organization rules may add or tune criteria, but every formal topic must have:

- a clear question or decision to examine;
- a defined scope and affected organization/jurisdiction;
- a neutral summary that can represent material disagreement;
- at least the minimum evidence/unknowns presentation required by the service;
- duplicate/lineage review;
- a consultation plan and dates;
- a named rule version and complete criteria trace; and
- no unresolved safety removal.

Moderators may correct formatting with author consent. They may not rewrite the proposal’s substance to make it easier to approve or defeat.

## Enforcement design principle

Use the least restrictive effective response: guidance, edit request, rate limit, temporary hold, visibility restriction, time-limited suspension, then removal or account restriction when necessary. Severity, recurrence, coordination, and risk can justify moving directly to stronger action. Every action must remain appealable unless law or immediate safety makes a temporary exception necessary; record that exception.
